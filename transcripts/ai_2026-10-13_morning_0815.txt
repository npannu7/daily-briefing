Sarah: This is exactly what our listener Nikhil was asking about in his feedback yesterday—independent developers taking consumer hardware and finding clever mechanical workarounds. But how did the Reddit community react? Because whenever someone claims they ran a frontier-sized model on a phone, half the comments usually assume it is snake oil.

Sarah: Sure, but offloading layers between two phones over a USB cord is a completely different engineering headache. In mechanical systems, if you couple two hydraulic cylinders to lift a single uneven load, you have to balance the fluid flow precisely. If one cylinder lags or starves of pressure, the whole linkage binds up. How did they balance the memory bandwidth across two completely different mobile system-on-chips without running out of memory?
Laura: That is where the actual mechanism gets fascinating. The developer split the workload into pipeline parallelism. Instead of each phone trying to hold the whole model, one phone takes seventy percent of the neural network layers and the other takes thirty percent. The master device processes its layers, passes the intermediate activation data over the USB connection, and the worker finishes the pass.
Sarah: So the USB cable is not transferring gigabytes of static model weights every step. It is only passing the small activation vector at the seam between the two sets of layers, like passing fluid pressure through a control valve between our paired actuators.
Laura: Exactly. That structural split drops the active memory footprint down to roughly four point four gigabytes on one phone and two point two gigabytes on the other. That easily fits inside their consumer memory limits without triggering out-of-memory crashes.
Sarah: But splitting the layers only solves half the problem. A twenty-seven-billion parameter model uncompressed is well over fifty gigabytes. Even standard four-bit compression would demand around fourteen gigabytes, which exceeds the memory on those phones. How did they compress it down to six and a half gigabytes total?
Laura: They used ternary quantization, which means compressing every weight down to just three possible values: minus one, zero, or positive one.
Sarah: That sounds like structural tolerance stepping in precision manufacturing. Instead of machining a continuous, infinite surface, you bin your parts into three rigid dimensional go-no-go buckets. Laura: Normally, yes. If you bluntly round weights to three values, the extreme outlier values get clipped and your language model starts spitting out absolute gibberish. But the author relied on a Hadamard transform. Think of it as a rigid geometric rotation applied to the mathematical weight space. It disperses those massive outlier spikes evenly across all dimensions, smoothing out the peaks before you snap everything into the three ternary bins.
Sarah: Ah, so instead of shaving off a massive burr on a machined surface, you distribute the stress evenly across the entire surface profile first.
Laura: Exactly. And the brilliant practical consequence on mobile silicon is arithmetic simplicity. When your weights are only minus one, zero, and one, you eliminate complex floating-point multiplication entirely. The processor only performs simple addition and subtraction. That removes the heavy multiply-accumulate operations that usually bake mobile chips.
Sarah: That explains the thermal claim. Because addition circuits pull a fraction of the switching energy compared to floating-point multipliers, the chip never hits the thermal threshold that triggers severe clock throttling.
Laura: Exactly. The developer measured the chassis temperature, and it peaked around thirty degrees Celsius. That is barely warm to the touch.

Sarah: That is only a one point five percent loss in perplexity. In practical terms, that means the model retains almost full conversational coherence while running on low-power mobile central processing units.
Laura: Precisely. So checking our two main claims: running partitioned across two consumer phones without thermal throttling is completely substantiated. And preserving sufficient functional precision via Hadamard ternary quantization is verified by the benchmark logs.
