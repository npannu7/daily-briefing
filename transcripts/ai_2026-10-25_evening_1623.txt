If your plan is governed by ERISA, state-law claims for bad-faith denial or medical malpractice by the plan get "preempted" — displaced — because Congress wanted one national rulebook, not fifty.

Davila didn't appeal, didn't pay out of pocket — just took the Naprosyn, had a severe reaction, ended up needing extensive treatment and hospitalization. The other plaintiff needed a hospital stay; CIGNA authorized only one night, she was discharged early, complications followed. Both sued under Texas state law for improper denial of care.
Sarah: And the Court said?
Laura: Unanimous. Justice Thomas wrote: ERISA's civil enforcement provision — section five zero two (a) — is the exclusive remedy. You can sue to recover benefits owed, but not for extra damages like pain and suffering or medical costs from the denial itself. The logic: if state tort claims were allowed, every denial becomes a potential lawsuit, plans get more expensive, employers drop coverage.
